


### **Question 5: Hospital/Item/Supplier Analysis**

**Relation Schema:**
$R(\text{hospitalName, itemDescription, supplierNo})$

**Data Table:**
| hospitalName | itemDescription | supplierNo |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Western General | Antiseptic Wipes | S1 |
| Western General | Paper Towels | S2 |
| Yorkhill | Antiseptic Wipes | S2 |
| Western General | Antiseptic Wipes | S2 |

---

### **Part (a): Explain why the relation is not in 5NF**

**The Concept of 5NF:**
A relation is in 5NF if it cannot be broken down into smaller pieces (projections) and then put back together (Natural Joined) without losing data.
If the relation $R$ is exactly equal to the Natural Join of three smaller projections, then $R$ has a "Join Dependency" and is **not** in 5NF.

**Step 1: Identify the Three Projections**
Let's look at the valid pairs existing in the single table:

1.  **$P_1$ (Hospital, Item):**
    *   (Western General, Antiseptic Wipes)
    *   (Western General, Paper Towels)
    *   (Yorkhill, Antiseptic Wipes)
2.  **$P_2$ (Item, Supplier):**
    *   (Antiseptic Wipes, S1)
    *   (Paper Towels, S2)
    *   (Antiseptic Wipes, S2)
3.  **$P_3$ (Hospital, Supplier):**
    *   (Western General, S1)
    *   (Western General, S2)
    *   (Yorkhill, S2)

**Step 2: Analyze the Join Behavior**
To prove the Join Dependency exists, we must see if $R = P_1 \bowtie P_2 \bowtie P_3$.
Let's trace what happens specifically with **Yorkhill** and **Antiseptic Wipes**.

1.  **Join $P_1$ and $P_2$:**
    *   **Input from $P_1$:** `(Yorkhill, Antiseptic Wipes)`
    *   **Matches in $P_2$:** This item matches **TWO** suppliers: `S1` and `S2`.
    *   **Result:** The Natural Join **creates 2 rows** because it finds all matches:
        1.  `(Yorkhill, Antiseptic Wipes, S1)`
        2.  `(Yorkhill, Antiseptic Wipes, S2)`

2.  **Join the Result with $P_3$:**
    *   Now we filter those 2 rows against $P_3$ (Hospital-Supplier).
    *   **Row 1:** `(Yorkhill, S1)` -> Does this pair exist in $P_3$? **No.** (Yorkhill only deals with S2). -> **Row Deleted.**
    *   **Row 2:** `(Yorkhill, S2)` -> Does this pair exist in $P_3$? **Yes.** -> **Row Kept.**

**Conclusion for Part A:**
The original table enforces a strict rule: a row only exists if it is valid in **all three** projections. Because the data integrity relies on this specific 3-way filtering process (where the join creates multiple possibilities and the 3rd table discards the invalid ones), the relation satisfies the Join Dependency $\bowtie \{P_1, P_2, P_3\}$. Since it holds this non-trivial dependency, it is **not in 5NF**.

### **Part (b): Normalize the relation to 5NF**

To normalize, we decompose the single relation into the three binary relations identified above.

**The 5NF Schemas:**

1.  **Table 1: Hospital-Item**
    *   Attributes: `(hospitalName, itemDescription)`
2.  **Table 2: Item-Supplier**
    *   Attributes: `(itemDescription, supplierNo)`
3.  **Table 3: Hospital-Supplier**
    *   Attributes: `(hospitalName, supplierNo)`

**Verification of the Decomposition:**
We verify this is a correct (lossless) decomposition by performing the **Natural Join** to reconstruct the original data.

**Scenario: Reconstructing data for Yorkhill**

1.  **Step 1 (Join Table 1 & 2):**
    *   Yorkhill needs Antiseptic Wipes.
    *   Antiseptic Wipes are supplied by **S1** and **S2**.
    *   The Join **creates two intermediate rows**:
        *   `(Yorkhill, Antiseptic Wipes, S1)`
        *   `(Yorkhill, Antiseptic Wipes, S2)`

2.  **Step 2 (Join with Table 3):**
    *   The Join checks the `Hospital-Supplier` table.
    *   It sees `(Yorkhill, S2)` exists.
    *   It sees `(Yorkhill, S1)` does **not** exist.
    *   It discards the S1 row and keeps the S2 row.

**Final Result:**
The query returns exactly one row: `(Yorkhill, Antiseptic Wipes, S2)`.
This perfectly matches the original data. The database is now in 5NF because the constraint is handled explicitly by the structure of the three tables.
